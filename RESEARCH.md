# RESEARCH — Polymarket BTC 5-minute Up/Down markets

Research log and evidence base for this project. Every factual claim about the market is
tagged with how it was verified. **Measured** means observed directly from live or historical
API data by code in this repo. **Docs** means read from docs.polymarket.com on 2026-11-24.
**Secondary** means press or third-party reporting, and is only used where it is labelled
as such. Empirical model results live in [MODEL_RESULTS.md](MODEL_RESULTS.md).

### 1.2 Settlement price: Chainlink Data Streams, 60-second TWAP

Current markets (since 2026-08-14) carry
`cryptoMarketConfig = {id: "btc-5m-twap-60", twapEnabled: true, twapLookbackSeconds: 60}` and
`resolutionSource = https://data.chain.link/streams/btc-usd-twap-60s-streams`. The description
says Up wins if "the time-weighted average price (TWAP) of Bitcoin, generated by Chainlink …
is greater than or equal to the price at the beginning of that range".

That sentence does not say which TWAP value counts, so we measured it. Polymarket's public
RTDS socket relays both the Chainlink BTC/USD spot stream (`crypto_prices_chainlink`) and the
60 s TWAP stream (`crypto_prices_twap_sixty`). We recorded both and compared them with the
official `eventMetadata` of resolved rounds (`scripts/verify_settlement.py`):

* **`priceToBeat` = TWAP-60 stream value at the round start second.** Absolute difference
  0.00000 on every round checked.
* **`finalPrice` = TWAP-60 stream value at the round end second.** Absolute difference
  0.00000 on every round checked. The spot stream at the end second differs from `finalPrice`
  by $11–$41, which confirms that settlement uses the TWAP, not spot.
* The TWAP-60 value at t ≈ the mean of the 1-second Chainlink spot samples over `[t-60, t)`:
  mean absolute error $0.52 over 263 seconds, versus $0.84 for `(t-60, t]`. It is not exact
  because Chainlink averages its own higher-frequency reports. The settlement therefore
  compares **two trailing 60-second averages**: the one ending at the round start and the one
  ending at the round end.

Consequences for prediction (all used by the model):

1. **The price to beat lags spot.** At the open, the current Chainlink spot generally differs
   from the price to beat, by however much BTC moved during the previous minute. So the
   outcome is not 50/50 at t = 0. This is fully determined by public information.
2. **The final value is an average.** Under a driftless random walk with per-second variance
   σ², the settlement value seen from time t has variance σ²·((T−60−t) + 60/3) for
   t ≤ T−60, and σ²·r³/(3·60²) inside the final window (r = seconds remaining). The effective
   horizon at t is therefore about T − t − 40 s, not T − t. This is implemented in
   `rules.effective_variance_time`, tested against simulation, and used for `z`.
3. The averaging window makes settlement much harder to move with a short burst of spot
   trading. That was the stated reason for the change (see §1.3).

Latency (measured from the RTDS stream): Chainlink spot and TWAP updates arrive a median
**1.5–1.6 s** (p95 ≈ 2.2 s) after their report timestamp. The official price to beat is
therefore observable about 1.6 s after the round opens. In this project **checkpoint k = 0
means "the instant the price to beat is first observable"** (tests enforce the corresponding
information horizon).

### 1.3 Settlement methodology history (account for it in every backtest)

Measured from all 80,603 rounds (`btc5m backfill-rounds --days 280`):

| Era | `cryptoMarketConfigId` | Resolution source | First round (UTC) | Last round (UTC) | Rounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|
| Spot | (none) / `btc-5m` | `data.chain.link/streams/btc-usd` | 2026-02-17 05:15 | 2026-08-06 23:55 | 49,173 |
| TWAP-30 | `btc-5m-twap-30` | `…/btc-usd-twap-30s-streams` | 2026-08-07 00:00 | 2026-08-13 23:55 | 2,016 |
| **TWAP-60** | `btc-5m-twap-60` | `…/btc-usd-twap-60s-streams` | **2026-08-14 00:00** | ongoing | 29,414 (to 2026-11-24 05:15) |

Secondary sources agree on the timing. Press reports (e.g. OneKey blog, Cointelegraph,
Forklog, SMU news) describe a July 2026 Stanford/SMU paper, *"Settlement Manipulation in
Prediction Markets"*, which found that the single-snapshot settlement of 5-minute BTC
contracts was exploitable. Forklog reports 821 wallets (0.34% of ~243k traders) earning
$8.22M in "anomalous cycles"; SMU and Cointelegraph quote a ~$1.28M wealth-transfer estimate.
The reports say Polymarket moved to TWAP settlement from 7 August 2026. The press disagreed
on 30 s versus 60 s windows; the market data shows both, in sequence.

**Policy in this repo:** models are trained and validated on the **TWAP-60 era only**. Earlier
eras are reported separately (parameter-free model only) because the target is a different
random variable.

### 1.4 Fees (measured per market, `feeSchedule`)

Fee formula (docs): `fee = C × feeRate × p × (1 − p)` for C shares at price p, charged to
**takers only**, rounded to 5 decimals. Makers pay nothing and are paid rebates.

| Period (round start, UTC) | rate | exponent | Taker fee at p = 0.50 |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 2026-02-17 → 2026-03-29 | 0.25 | 2 | see caveat |
| 2026-03-30 → 2026-11-20 16:10 | 0.01 | 1 | 0.25¢/share (0.5% of notional) |
| **2026-11-20 16:15 → now** | **0.05** | 1 | **1.25¢/share (2.5% of notional)** |

One round on 2026-08-05 14:55 carried 0.05; this looks like a one-off. Caveat: the current
docs only define the exponent-1 formula. For the exponent-2 era we apply
`C × rate × (p(1−p))^exponent`, which is unverified, but that era is outside the TWAP-60
training window. The fee was **raised 5× four days before this research**. All forward-looking
cost estimates use the current 0.05 schedule. Historical-fee results are reported alongside,
never instead.

Break-even: buying at p = 0.50 needs a true win probability above 0.50 + 0.0125 + spread
+ slippage. With a typical 1¢ spread and 1 tick of slippage, that is **≥ ~52.3%**, given an
accurate fill at the touch.

## 2. Data sources and API verification

Full catalogue with endpoints, limits and legal notes: [DATA_SOURCES.md](DATA_SOURCES.md). Key
verified facts:

* **Gamma** `GET /events?slug=…` accepts repeated `slug` params (20 events per call) and
  returns `eventMetadata.{priceToBeat, finalPrice}`, `outcomePrices`, `feeSchedule`,
  `cryptoMarketConfig` and `clobTokenIds`. `eventMetadata` appears only after resolution,
  which takes ~20 s to a few minutes after the round ends.
* **CLOB market WebSocket** (`wss://ws-subscriptions-clob.polymarket.com/ws/market`): `book`,
  `price_change`, `last_trade_price` (taker side), `best_bid_ask`, `market_resolved`. Public.
* **RTDS** (`wss://ws-live-data.polymarket.com`): public Chainlink spot and TWAP-60 for BTC.
  It is **deprecated** (docs: "Migrate from RTDS to PolyBolt"). The filter string must be
  compact JSON `{"symbol":"btc/usd"}`; with a space after the colon the server sends no
  updates (measured).
* **PolyBolt** (`wss://ws-live-v2.polymarket.com/ws`): the replacement. Price channels need
  CLOB API credentials and a Chainlink API key. Unauthenticated subscribe → `auth_required`
  (measured). This project never asks for private keys, so PolyBolt is optional and disabled
  by default.
* **Data API v2** `GET /v2/trades?condition=…` (≤ 20 condition ids, cursor pagination,
  `taker_only=true` default) gives every fill once, on its taker side, with **block
  timestamps**. Matched against our own WebSocket captures (4,410 trades), block timestamps
  lag the match time by a **median 2.2 s** (p05 1.2 s). Historical market lookups are
  shifted by +2 s, which errs in the market's favour. WS and API taker side agree on 98% of
  matched trades; the residue is likely mis-matches on duplicate price/size keys.
* **Price history**: `/v2/prices-history` keeps 1-minute resolution only ~7 days, so it is
  not usable for intra-round history. There is **no public historical order-book endpoint**.

## 3. Reconstructing history without paid Chainlink data

Intra-round Chainlink paths are not available historically, so the reference price is
reconstructed as `ref[s] = kraken_close[s] + basis`, with
`basis = priceToBeat − mean(kraken_close over [start−60, start))`. This uses only the official
price to beat and pre-start exchange data, so it adds no look-ahead. Quality is measured on
every round by reconstructing the official `finalPrice` and the outcome. In the first 859
rounds it **reproduced the official outcome in 98.95% of rounds** (final-price error sd ≈
$4.6). Full-sample figures are in MODEL_RESULTS.md. The ~1% of rounds where the proxy
disagrees are near-ties, which are inherently unpredictable at early checkpoints anyway.

Live, the engine uses the real Chainlink stream and also logs a proxy-based prediction for
every ledger record, so any train/serve skew can be measured on unseen rounds.

## 4. Literature review (verified citations)

**Order flow and microstructure**

* Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov (2014), *The Price Impact of Order Book Events*, J. Financial
  Econometrics 12(1):47–88 (arXiv:1011.6402). Over short intervals, price changes are driven
  mainly by **order-flow imbalance** (OFI) at the best quotes, linearly, with slope inversely
  proportional to depth. Note that this is a *contemporaneous* relation; it does not by
  itself imply forecasting power.
* Stoikov (2018), *The micro-price: a high-frequency estimator of future prices*, Quantitative
  Finance 18(12):1959–1966. The micro-price (mid adjusted for spread and imbalance) predicts
  short-term mid changes better than the mid or the weighted mid.
* Kolm, Turiel & Westray (2023), *Deep order flow imbalance: Extracting alpha at multiple
  horizons from the limit order book*, Mathematical Finance 33(4):1044–1081. Order-flow
  inputs beat raw book states. The useful horizon is about **two average price changes**,
  i.e. seconds, not minutes, for liquid assets.
* Makarov & Schoar (2020), *Trading and arbitrage in cryptocurrency markets*, J. Financial
  Economics 135(2):293–319. Signed volume has a common component that explains ~80% of
  bitcoin returns; cross-exchange deviations are mostly cross-country and capital-control
  driven, and small within the US.

**Short-horizon crypto prediction**

* Jaquart, Dann & Weinhardt (2021), *Short-term bitcoin market prediction via machine
  learning*, J. Finance and Data Science 7:45–66. 1–60 minute horizons. All models beat a
  random classifier and GRU/GBM do best, **but a long-short strategy is negative after
  transaction costs**. This is the base rate this project should expect: weak statistical
  predictability that does not survive costs.

**Prediction-market microstructure (Polymarket)**

* Dubach (2026), *The Anatomy of a Decentralized Prediction Market* (arXiv:2604.24366). A
  52-day tick archive. Trade direction inferred from the public book feed agrees with
  on-chain truth only ~59% of the time, so aggressor side should come from fills. We use
  taker-side fills from the Data API.
* Qin & Yang (2026), *Polymarket-v1 Database* (arXiv:2606.04217). 1.2B trades with
  ground-truth aggressor direction. Tick-rule classification is near-random on Polymarket.
* Jia et al. (2026), *Unlocking the Forecasting Economy* (arXiv:2604.20421). Full-lifecycle
  datasets.
* Industry reporting (Messari, citing a Dune study; secondary, unverified): bots account for
  ~55% of 5-minute and ~62% of 15-minute crypto market participation. Practitioner blogs
  describe "latency arbitrage" against stale Polymarket quotes. Polymarket introduced taker
  fees on these markets specifically to blunt that (FinanceMagnates, secondary).

**Methodology**

* López de Prado (2018), *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*: purging, embargo,
  walk-forward evaluation.
* Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu (2017), *The Probability of Backtest Overfitting*,
  J. Computational Finance. Bailey & López de Prado (2014), *The Deflated Sharpe Ratio*. Both
  warn about selection bias from testing many strategy variants. We report every variant
  and threshold tried, not the best one.
* Proper scoring rules (Brier, log loss) and reliability diagrams are used instead of
  accuracy alone, because a probability forecast is only useful for pricing a binary
  contract if it is calibrated.

## 5. Limitations of existing crypto-prediction work (and how this repo addresses them)

| Common flaw | Mitigation here |
|---|---|
| Random K-fold splits on time series (leakage through autocorrelation) | Chronological walk-forward only, with a 1 h purge between train/val/test; all checkpoints of a round stay together |
| Evaluating accuracy, not calibration | Brier, log loss, ECE and reliability tables per checkpoint |
| Ignoring settlement mechanics (TWAP vs spot, ties, lagged price to beat) | Settlement-exact target and TWAP-aware variance; eras separated |
| Ignoring costs, spread and latency | Fee schedule per market plus the current fee, fills from the first observed print after 2–5 s latency, slippage, skipped rounds when no liquidity is evidenced |
| Look-ahead through timestamps | Point-in-time feature builder shared with live code; leakage unit tests; block-timestamp offset measured and corrected |
| Comparing against a coin flip instead of the market | Primary benchmark is Polymarket's own implied probability |
| Reporting the best of many variants | All models, thresholds and checkpoints are reported; promotion gate requires significance at several checkpoints |

## 6. Hypotheses tested

H1. Early in the round (k ≤ 60 s), public information (distance to the lagged price to beat,
volatility, TWAP geometry) predicts the outcome better than a coin flip. *Expected: yes,
mechanically.*

H2. Microstructure features (order-flow imbalance, momentum, mean reversion, regimes,
liquidity) add out-of-sample predictive power beyond the volatility-adjusted distance `z`.

H3. A model beats **Polymarket's market-implied probability** at the same time, on unseen
rounds.

H4. Any such edge survives fees, spread, latency and slippage.

Results are in [MODEL_RESULTS.md](MODEL_RESULTS.md). Live-only hypotheses (order-book
imbalance, the gap between exchanges and the lagging Chainlink stream, futures basis,
liquidations) are logged on every ledger record and need the live paper test for a verdict;
no historical data exists for them.
